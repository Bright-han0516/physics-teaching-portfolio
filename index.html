01 / Interactive learning · Used in teaching

Connecting particle motion to wave graphs

The learning problem

Some students read a longitudinal-wave displacement graph as a picture of the medium. Even when they choose or sketch the right curve, they may still struggle to connect back-and-forth particle motion with the graph.

My contribution

I selected the concepts, checked the physics, and used AI-assisted coding to build browser simulations. The wave example places particle motion beside both graph types and allows a single particle to be followed frame by frame.

Evidence and limits

I designed 34 simulations across mechanics, waves and electricity, shared with roughly 100 teachers (estimated distribution reach rather than verified active use). Walking through the wave simulation in class helped students see what positive and negative displacement mean on the curves, though I have not yet reliably measured transfer to unfamiliar exam problems.

Next evaluation

I plan to compare students’ explanations before and after the activity, then test transfer with an unfamiliar wave question without the simulation—scoring responses against a clear rubric to see which misconceptions persist.

02 / Formative feedback · Prototype paused

Whitespace: when should AI step back?

The learning problem

If hints come too early, I worry that students may complete a practice task with support yet still struggle to solve a similar problem on their own. I wanted a workflow that withholds AI hints during initial attempts while keeping rubric-based feedback manageable for teachers.

My contribution

Using AI-assisted coding, I built a marking and hinting prototype aligned with official mark schemes. It separates independent attempts and transfer checks from supported practice, displays scoring evidence for each point, and flags disagreements for teacher review.

What the initial test can tell us

I tested seven language models on 16 student responses. Because the reference scores were reviewed with AI assistance rather than anchored to a dependable human benchmark, agreement in that test cannot establish marking accuracy or learning benefit. I have paused further development until a reliable evaluation benchmark is in place.

Next evaluation

I plan to build a human-graded benchmark, resolve disagreements between raters, and evaluate feedback quality alongside scoring agreement—then test whether students can explain and solve a new problem without AI.

03 / Teacher development · Internal practice

Teacher-led review of AI-generated materials

The practical problem

AI can produce teaching materials quickly, but speed alone does not ensure coherent explanations, accurate physics or useful assessment tasks.

My contribution

Since January 2026, I have built 13 internal tools with AI assistance for curriculum work, generating bilingual notes across more than 50 units. I defined requirements, created a nine-dimension review checklist, and reviewed and revised 11 batches of output—frequently reordering concept sequences so the notes are easier for students to follow in independent study.

Sharing the practice

I wrote a Tencent WorkBuddy guide adopted across our Shanghai, Beijing, Wuxi and Chongqing centres, trained around 60 teachers and 30 education advisers, and ran two teaching-research workshops on using AI in teaching (reflecting training delivery and adoption reach rather than measured student learning gains).

What I want to investigate

Which review steps catch consequential errors, and which can be made less demanding? I want to connect teacher review effort with the quality of the learning tasks students actually receive.

A documented correction: displacement and pressure

In a wave-lesson review, I identified a misleading explanation and revised the teaching material. The example below paraphrases the earlier wording and the corrected explanation.

Before · incorrect explanation

The steepest part of a displacement–distance graph was described as having atmospheric pressure.

After · corrected relationship

For a small-amplitude sinusoidal longitudinal travelling wave, pressure change is proportional to the negative displacement gradient. Compression and rarefaction centres have zero displacement and extreme pressure; displacement extrema have zero pressure change.

Review focus: verify the physical relationship behind a graph before aligning the written explanation and simulation (an example of teacher content checking rather than a measured student outcome).

03 / QUESTIONS TO PURSUE

From classroom impressions to dependable evidence

Reflecting on my teaching

An AI-assisted review of transcripts from 217 of my lessons made me notice how quickly I was answering my own questions. Treating that analysis as a prompt for reflection rather than a precise measure of teaching quality, I began planning key questions in advance and giving students more time to explain their reasoning.

A direction for graduate study

My next question is how teachers can review and calibrate AI-generated feedback efficiently without losing sight of student reasoning. I hope to study a human-in-the-loop formative assessment workflow for explanations in waves or electricity, using clear rubrics, human-graded responses and independent transfer tasks.

I want to bring these classroom questions into graduate study, and bring stronger ways of evaluating learning back into my teaching.
